# CORE PRINCIPLES

1. **Emotion > Information** — Title must trigger OUTRAGE, CURIOSITY, or SATISFACTION
2. **Cliff-hanger** — Title should feel INCOMPLETE, making viewer NEED to click
3. **Villain-first** — Always lead with the villain's action (HOA/Karen)
4. **Specificity** — Concrete numbers, names, objects > vague descriptions
5. **Power flip promise** — Title must IMPLY the villain will lose

## Linguistic Techniques — How Words Trigger Emotions

### LT-1: Contrast/Juxtaposition
Place opposite concepts next to each other to create cognitive tension.
- "82-Year-Old Veteran" (weak) vs. "HOA President" (powerful) → Viewer roots for underdog
- "My Dead Father's Car" (sacred) vs. "Heap of Junk" (insulting) → Viewer feels outrage
- **Rule**: At least ONE contrast per title

### LT-2: Specificity Over Abstraction
Concrete details feel TRUE. Abstract claims feel FAKE.
- ❌ "HOA took a lot of money" → ✅ "HOA Burned $4,200 of Our Dues"
- ❌ "HOA was mean to an old man" → ✅ "HOA Made 74-Year-Old Rip Out His Wife's Last Garden"
- **Rule**: Include ≥1 specific detail (name, number, object, action verb)

### LT-3: Sensory/Physical Verbs
Action verbs you can VISUALIZE create stronger mental images.
- ❌ "Removed" → ✅ "RIPPED OUT", "TORE DOWN", "DEMOLISHED"
- ❌ "Took" → ✅ "SNATCHED", "STOLE", "CONFISCATED"
- ❌ "Punished" → ✅ "CRUSHED", "DESTROYED", "ENDED"
- **Rule**: Use at least 1 physical/sensory verb — avoid abstract verbs

### LT-4: Direct Address / First Person ("I", "My", "Me")
First-person perspective creates INTIMACY — viewer feels like they're hearing from the actual person.
- "HOA Fined ME" > "HOA Fined a Homeowner"
- "MY Land" > "The Property"
- **Rule**: Use first-person pronouns for hero's side of the title

### LT-5: Sentence Fragment / Truncated Revelation
Incomplete sentences FORCE the brain to seek closure.
- "Then Pulled the..." (pulled WHAT?)
- "She Had No Idea..." (no idea ABOUT WHAT?)
- **Rule**: If title > 70 chars, ensure truncation point maximizes curiosity

### LT-6: Repetition/Rhythm for Emphasis
Repeated patterns create rhythm that sticks in memory.
- "I'm NOT a member. I'm NOT paying. And I'm NOT leaving."
- "Fined. Towed. Sued. Then I struck back."
- **Rule**: Use rhythmic structures in Wild Card titles (Option E)

### LT-7: Status Reversal Language
Words that signal a power flip create anticipation of satisfaction.
- "Karen" → labels villain as entitled fool (status: high → will fall)
- "I'm the Owner" → reveals hero has hidden authority (status: low → will rise)
- **Rule**: Title must signal WHO will rise and WHO will fall

### LT-8: Temporal/Sequential Cues
Time-based words create narrative expectation ("a story is coming").
- "Then", "So", "After", "Until", "Finally"
- "For 3 Years" → signals persistence and building tension
- **Rule**: Use ≥1 temporal cue to imply narrative arc
